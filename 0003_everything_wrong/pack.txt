Technique: intro = spoken frame (narrator now, outside the
scene). Verse = answering voice (parenthetical second presence,
diegetic — was in the room, remembers, knows more than the
lead did). Pre-chorus = voice stacking. Chorus = open. Bridge
= compression. Final chorus = after-bridge. Outro = return to
spoken frame, then understatement.

Notation: [brackets] = directions, not sung. [ref:] =
plain-English helper, not sung. Parentheses with vowel hums
(oo, ah, mm, oh) = filler, sung. Parentheses with words =
answering voice, sung by a second voice, verse only.

Fillers: Intro = spoken, no fillers. Pre-chorus keeps fillers.
Chorus = open, no fillers — it formulates, it does not breathe.
Verse = answering voice replaces fillers. Bridge = no fillers
— that's what makes it fast. Outro = spoken frame opens, then
no fillers, decay.

APPENDIX — DELETE WHEN REMIXING

PHONETIC / SAFE-VARIANTS (bridge):
Compressed lines read as one continuous phrase. Removed vowels
are silent; consonants glue to the next word. [ref:] above
gives intended word breaks — read across without pausing. The
rhythm never changes. Merge only where a syllable is saved.
One-syllable function words (and, then, I, am) stay separate.
Merging must not produce a different English word (aversion
≠ a version); if the merged form is itself a word, drop the
leading vowel (a version → vrsion). If a model glitches,
restore the shortest readable form (is → 's → s; "a door" →
ador → adoor; every → evry). Keep syllable count, keep stress
pattern. Never lengthen the rhythm. Unzip one letter at a
time until the glitch clears.

ANSWERING VOICE (verse):
The text in parentheses with words is not an echo of the
lead. It is a second, diegetic presence — a voice that was in
the room when the scene happened, and that remembers it. It
knows more than the lead did at the time, or it knows the
same thing differently. It answers, extends, or contradicts —
it never repeats the lead verbatim. In this pack it is the
coach: warm, unhurried; asks back or extends the thought, and
never argues. One to four words per answer; longer answers
turn the technique into dialogue and break the rhythm. The
answer lands in a different register or pan than the lead —
lower, left-panned. If it flattens into the lead line, the
technique fails. Parentheses with vowel hums (oo, ah, mm, oh)
are fillers, not answers — they follow the filler rule for
their section.

AFTER-BRIDGE (final chorus):
Final chorus repeats earlier choruses verbatim. Only delivery
changes: belt from the first line, added harmony, layered
vocals.

VOICE STACKING (pre-chorus):
Pre-chorus builds by adding voices, not volume. Line 1 = one
voice; each next line adds one or more distinct voices —
different register, pan, or echo. Last line = full unison
stack. Never double the lead verbatim.

UNDERSTATEMENT (outro):
Outro does not conclude — it decays. Voice stops finishing
words, lines, thoughts. Silence becomes content. Cut where
the voice would naturally run out. Never lengthen. If a
fragment returns, it returns smaller. Read as one continuous
decay.

NOTES:
Bridge has no fillers — that's what makes it fast.
[brackets] and [ref:] are not sung.
