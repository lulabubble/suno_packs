Technique: intro spoken frame, coastal British. Verse =
answering voice, diegetic-to-algorithmic shift; instrumental
answer (bass + shell tone in V1, error tone + buffer stutter
in V2). Accent paradox = coastal British where water is,
neutral English where digital harbor takes over. Phonetic
nudge = respelling. Pre-chorus = wave. Chorus = open. Bridge
= compression. FC = after-bridge, thesis flip; last line
becomes two lines of call and response. Outro = submersion,
accent dissolves last. Production as map = analog vs digital.

APPENDIX — DELETE WHEN REMIXING

PHONETIC / SAFE-VARIANTS (bridge):
Compressed lines read as one continuous phrase. [ref:] gives
intended word breaks. Merge only saving a syllable.
One-syllable function words (and, then, I, am) stay apart.
Merge must not form a different English word; if it does,
drop the leading vowel. "asea" and "alido" = "a sea" and
"a lido" — read as two words each. Keep syllable count and
stress pattern; never lengthen rhythm.

ACCENT PARADOX (whole track):
Same language, two worlds. Coastal British = real sea.
Neutral English = digital harbor. Accent shifts with the
water. Bridge: neutral, cracks back to coastal on last line.
FC: coastal in full; last line call-and-response — coastal
claims wave, neutral claims shell; not merged. Outro: accent
dissolves last, after words float. Nudge: respell where
British and American differ — wata, learnt, lido; "harbor"
as counter-marker.

ANSWERING VOICE (verse) — DIEGETIC-TO-ALGORITHMIC SHIFT:
(word) = second diegetic presence, not an echo. V1: older
local, coastal British, warm, knows more than the lead did
then; instrumental answer (bass, shell tone) in same
diegesis. V2: algorithmic — flat, neutral, hard-left, on the
beat, repeats or distorts; replaced by digital artifact. One
to four words per answer.
