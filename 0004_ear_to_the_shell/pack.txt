Technique: intro spoken frame, coastal British. Verse =
answering voice, diegetic-to-algorithmic shift; instrumental
answer (bass + shell tone in V1, error tone + buffer stutter
in V2). Accent paradox = coastal British where water is,
neutral English where digital harbor takes over. Phonetic
nudge = respelling. Pre-chorus = wave. Chorus = open. Bridge
= compression. FC = after-bridge, thesis flip; last line
becomes two lines of call and response. Outro = submersion,
accent dissolves last. Production as map = analog vs digital.

Notation / fillers: [brackets] and [ref:] not sung. (vowel
hums) = filler, sung. (words) = answering voice, verse only.
[spoken] = spoken. [hum] = hummed. Intro spoken, no fillers.
Pre-chorus keeps fillers. Chorus none. Verse = answering
voice replaces them. Bridge none. Outro none after spoken.

Accent rule: coastal British = real sea. Neutral English =
digital harbor. Shift between sections is the technique.

APPENDIX — DELETE WHEN REMIXING

PHONETIC / SAFE-VARIANTS (bridge):
Compressed lines read as one continuous phrase. [ref:] above
gives intended word breaks — read across without pausing.
Merge only where a syllable is saved. One-syllable function
words (and, then, I, am) stay separate. Merging must not
produce a different English word (aversion ≠ a version); if
the merged form is itself a word, drop the leading vowel (a
version → vrsion). "asea" and "alido" are merged forms of "a
sea" and "a lido" — read as two words each. If a model
glitches, restore the shortest readable form (is → 's → s;
every → evry). Keep syllable count, keep stress pattern.
Never lengthen the rhythm. Unzip one letter at a time.

PHONETIC NUDGE (coastal British sections):
Directive alone is not enough — Suno predicts phonemes from
text. Respell a small number of words where British and
American pronunciation differ, and where respelling is also
a compression. Applied here: wata (water, drops two letters,
marks non-rhotic R), learnt (learned), lido (public open-air
bath, British lexis). V2 uses "harbor" (American spelling)
as counter-marker. Do not respell words identical in both
accents (tide, shell, sound, keep). If the model glitches,
restore shortest readable version (wata → watah → water).

ACCENT PARADOX (whole track):
Same language, two worlds. Coastal British = real sea.
Neutral English = digital harbor. Accent shifts with the
water. In the bridge, accent strips to neutral and cracks
back to coastal on the last line only. In FC, coastal
returns in full; last line is call and response — coastal
claims the wave, neutral claims the shell; they do not
merge. In outro, accent dissolves last, after words are
gone.

ANSWERING VOICE (verse) — DIEGETIC-TO-ALGORITHMIC SHIFT:
Parentheses with words = second diegetic presence, not an
echo. V1: local, older, deeper coastal British, warm,
unhurried, knows more than the lead did then; instrumental
answer (bass, shell tone) in same diegesis. V2: algorithmic
— flat, neutral English, no accent, hard-left, arrives on
the beat, repeats or distorts; instrumental answer replaced
by digital artifact (error tone, buffer stutter). One to
four words per answer. If shift is not audible between V1
and V2, technique fails. (Vowel hums) are fillers.

WAVE (pre-chorus):
Builds in a wave shape, not a straight ramp. Line 1 = one
voice, quiet. Line 2 = two voices, octave up. Line 3 = back
to one voice, louder and closer — the pull-back. Line 4 =
full stack, release. The retreat in line 3 is what makes
line 4 land. Never double the lead verbatim.

AFTER-BRIDGE (final chorus):
FC repeats earlier choruses verbatim, except last line. In
this pack the last line is replaced by two lines of call
and response: coastal claims "I am the wave", neutral claims
"I am the shell". They do not merge. Delivery: belt from
line 1, added harmony. Coastal British returns in full.

SUBMERSION (outro):
Does not conclude — sinks. Voice stops finishing words, then
thoughts, then stops forming words at all. Hums and pauses
carry the content. Accent dissolves last, after words are
gone. Never lengthen. Track feels like an ear slowly lifted
from a shell.

PRODUCTION AS MAP (all sections):
Section headers carry production, not melody. Coastal
sections: tape hiss, spring reverb, analog warmth, shell
tone. Neutral sections: bit-crush, gated reverb, compression
artifacts, error tone. Listener hears where they are before
parsing words. If V1 and V2 sound like the same room,
technique fails.

DEFERRED (post-test, not fixed preemptively):
- Chorus 2 is textually identical to Chorus 1 by design.
  Drift is a bet on delivery, not on words. If the test
  shows no drift, change one word in Chorus 2 ("sound" →
  "noise"), keep "wata" as anchor.
- Outro accent may vanish with words, not after them. If
  the test shows this, add one coastal whisper before the
  first hum.
- PC1 "I keep the ones that are worth keeping" — tautology
  kept for the pull-back of the wave. If the test shows it
  drags, drop "that are".
- V1 "forecast reads it" — inverted logic kept. If the test
  shows it breaks, replace with "the way the forecast
  reads the sea".
- Bridge compression at the limit. If it breaks at speed,
  unzip one letter at a time, starting with
  "Citythatwanted asea builtalido".
